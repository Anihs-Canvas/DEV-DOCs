BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT
--------------------
Nothing in this proposal changes the safepicks or basketballpicks plan. Three of
the four items are already-proven-dead against what this project has established.
The fourth (sentiment) is the only one with a pulse, and on inspection it collapses
into the lever we ALREADY identified — early-line CLV / devig line-shopping against
soft books — while adding a noisy, unvalidated feature on top. It is not a new
information edge over the sharp closing line.

Grounding (treated as established, not re-derived):
- Architecture is not the lever. GBTs (LightGBM/XGBoost) beat deep nets on tabular
  data under ~10^5 rows (Grinsztajn 2022; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon 2022). In the 2023
  Soccer Prediction Challenge the bookmaker consensus beat EVERY entrant, incl. DL.
- Our stack (Dixon-Coles + LightGBM + real xG + isotonic calibration) already reaches
  the Pinnacle closing line to within +/-0.001 nats and does NOT beat it.
- The ceiling is INFORMATION, not model power. The residual gap = info the close
  already prices (lineups, injuries, sharp money). A better model only makes us more
  market-GRADE, never market-beating.
- basketballpicks: the one live edge is player props / DFS via devig line-shopping,
  not model superiority.

PROPOSAL 1 — Pre-trained soccer transformers ("Podos Soccer Model")
================================================================================
VERDICT: already-proven-dead (both soccer and basketball).

VERIFIED (the model is real):
- Bettensor/podos_soccer_model exists on HF (mirror: Nickel5HF/podos_soccer_model).
- It is a 276K-parameter "small baseline" transformer. Trained on 100,000 games /
  569 teams from football-data.co.uk. 23 input parameters.
- CRITICAL FACT: its inputs INCLUDE the sportsbook odds (home/draw/away) plus shots,
  shots on target, corners, offsides, cards, streaks/form.
- It makes NO accuracy or profitability claim. Stated limitations: "home team bias"
  and "overconfident scores." It is explicitly a v1 baseline.
- Context: "Bettensor" is a Bittensor crypto subnet incentivizing betting predictions;
  this is a community baseline artifact, not a validated market-beating model.

WHY IT'S DEAD:
- A model FED the bookmaker odds as a feature cannot systematically beat the market it
  is derived from; at best it re-expresses the odds it was handed. That is the exact
  ceiling we already hit, minus our advantages.
- It is strictly weaker than our stack: no isotonic calibration, no Dixon-Coles
  low-score correction, no real xG, tiny params, admitted home bias + overconfidence
  (i.e. mis-calibrated — the one thing our pipeline explicitly fixes).
- 569-team encoder does not cover our 16-league catalogue cleanly; no player data.
- Basketball: irrelevant — soccer-specific, and the same "fed-the-odds baseline"
  logic applies to any analogous pre-trained hoops transformer.

Nothing here to test. Skip.

================================================================================
PROPOSAL 2 — Probabilistic time-series transformers (over match history / xG)
================================================================================
VERDICT: already-proven-dead (both).

- This is the HF Time-Series Transformer / Informer / Autoformer family. Our data is
  tabular, engineered match features in the ~10^4–10^5 row regime — precisely where
  GBTs beat deep nets (Grinsztajn 2022; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon 2022).
- A sequence transformer over match/xG history would, best case, MATCH LightGBM on
  engineered rolling/form features; realistically it underperforms and is far hungrier
  for data and tuning. Our per-league, per-season row counts (esp. the summer/xG
  deserts already documented) are nowhere near transformer-favourable scale.
- Even a perfect sequence model cannot break the INFORMATION ceiling. Match-history/xG
  is exactly the goals-model information the close already contains. More model power
  on the same information = more market-grade, not market-beating. This is the project's
  core, already-proven finding.
- Basketball: identical logic; tabular box-score/pace features, GBT territory.

No edge. Skip.

================================================================================
PROPOSAL 3 — Sentiment / social-media analysis (SportsBERT -> XGBoost)  [MAIN EFFORT]
================================================================================
VERDICT: worth-a-narrow-test in theory, but the only viable pocket is ALREADY our
plan (early-line CLV / devig vs soft books). As a distinct new lever: dead. It does
NOT change strategy.

The precise question: does public/news/forum sentiment carry information the SHARP
CLOSING LINE does not already contain?
ANSWER: No — not against the close. Where sentiment "works," it is a PRICING distortion
on SOFT/recreational books BEFORE convergence, i.e. a soft-line-vs-sharp-line timing
play, not new predictive information. And you capture that distortion better by reading
the soft line directly (devig) than by scoring tweets.

TECHNICAL REALITY CHECK ON THE PROPOSAL ITSELF:
- microsoft/SportsBERT is real, but it is a FILL-MASK base model (masked-LM), NOT a
  sentiment classifier. It outputs ranked token predictions, not sentiment labels. The
  card explicitly says fine-tune it "for other tasks such as classification." So the
  pitch's "run tweets through SportsBERT to gauge sentiment" is wrong out of the box —
  you'd first need a LABELED sports-sentiment corpus (which we don't have) to fine-tune,
  then still validate that the resulting feature beats the line. That's a large build
  for a feature the evidence says is already priced.

WHAT THE PUBLISHED EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SAYS:
- Feddersen, Humphreys & Soebbing, "Sentiment Bias and Asset Prices" (Economic Inquiry,
  2017; 7 pro leagues, Facebook-Likes as sentiment proxy): bookmakers SHADE prices toward
  popular/high-sentiment teams, BUT "favorable prices do not translate into a higher
  probability of winning a bet." Translation: sentiment creates a price bias you would
  FADE, not follow — and the mispricing lives in the posted (soft) line, not as fresh
  outcome-predictive signal. Same team extended this to the NBA (J. Sports Economics).
- Favorite-longshot / public-bias literature (Levitt 2004; Paul & Weinbach 2007;
  Forrest & Simmons 2008; Franck et al. 2011): public overbets favorites and overs;
  soft books shade lines to match; contrarian value ("fade the public inflated favorite/
  over") exists ON THE SOFT LINE and is arbitraged out BY THE SHARP CLOSE. Results are
  mixed across studies — not a robust standalone alpha.
- "Prediction from regional angst" (NFL Twitter, Decision Support Systems, 2017):
  reports a "golden cross" sentiment pattern returning ~$14.84/wager vs -$12.21 odds-only,
  and ~$48.18/wager on 41 underdog bets. BUT these are small-sample, in-sample results
  against POSTED lines; the surrounding literature notes variables that move both opening
  AND closing lines do not predict outcomes, and NO out-of-sample profitable strategy
  survives. Classic in-sample mining; does not clear the sharp-close bar.

SYNTHESIS FOR THIS PROJECT:
- Team news / injuries / morale — the real signal inside "sentiment" — is read by sharps
  and priced into Pinnacle's close within seconds. Against the close, sentiment is redundant.
- The ONLY exploitable pocket is behavioral and EARLY: recreational sentiment inflates a
  SOFT book's favorite/star/over before it converges to the sharp number. That is real,
  but it is exactly the mechanism the project already identified as its one untested path:
  early-line CLV via The-Odds-API / devig line-shopping. In that pocket, the dominant,
  lower-variance signal is the soft price itself vs the sharp price — a sentiment score is
  a noisy proxy for a number you can read directly. Sentiment does not add information; it
  restates, with noise, a distortion you already measure by devigging.

EXACT NARROW CONDITION (if ever tested): sentiment could only earn its keep as an EARLY
TRIGGER to flag which soft lines are most sentiment-inflated, measured strictly by realized
CLV against the sharp close — never as a direct outcome predictor, never fed to a model that
outputs win probabilities. Even then it is a marginal add-on to the devig plan, not a new lever.
Priority: low. Do the devig/early-line CLV work first (already planned); only revisit sentiment
if devig proves the soft-line pocket exists AND you can show sentiment improves selection within it.

BASKETBALL SPECIFICS:
- NBA has strong public/star-name bias (documented by the same Feddersen line of work), so
  the soft-line distortion is if anything larger — but you monetize it the same way: devig
  line-shopping on player props / DFS across soft books at the moment of the soft price. A
  SportsBERT sentiment feature is strictly worse than reading the prop line differential and
  would need its own labeled training data. Same verdict: subsumed by the devig plan, not new.

DOES ANY OF THIS CHANGE THE PLAN? No.
- safepicks: no change. Keep the calibrated DC + LightGBM + xG stack; the residual gap is
  information, not architecture. The only live path remains early-line CLV via The-Odds-API.
- basketballpicks: no change. The edge remains player props / DFS devig line-shopping.
- Sentiment is NOT an information edge over the sharp close. It is largely already in the
  price; where it isn't, the distortion is soft-book/early and is captured better and more
  cheaply by devig than by any transformer sentiment feature.

Owner's-honesty note: the write-up in leagues_january_to_march.txt reads like a generic
LLM "yes you can build this" answer. Every item is technically buildable and none of it
moves the profitability needle. SportsBERT isn't even a sentiment classifier as pitched.
