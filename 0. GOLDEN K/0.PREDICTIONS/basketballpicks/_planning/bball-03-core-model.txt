--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(0) THE ONE-PARAGRAPH FRAME (why this model exists and what it must NOT promise)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The feasibility verdict is binding: we will NOT beat the sharp NBA closing line
on spread/moneyline/total with a public-data model — a pace-efficiency model goes
MARKET-GRADE and stalls at the same information ceiling the soccer goals model
hit (lineups, minutes intent, late injury/load-management, sharp flow). So the
CORE team model is NOT the product. It is INFRASTRUCTURE with two honest jobs:
  (1) be a calibrated, market-grade forecaster we can trust as a sanity anchor
      and a devig cross-check (know when a soft book/DFS line is genuinely off
      vs when WE are the one who is wrong); and
  (2) produce the PLAYER-LEVEL distributions (minutes → usage → per-stat) that
      FEED the props edge — the one genuinely-live +EV door (soft books + DFS
      pick'em, especially neglected stats and WNBA/college).
Every design choice below serves "calibrated and honest," never "beats the
market." We inherit safepicks' entire calibration-first, leakage-paranoid,
seal-the-holdout doctrine and port it wholesale; we replace the Poisson/
Dixon-Coles GENERATIVE core because basketball is a different stochastic object.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) WHY NOT POISSON / DIXON-COLES — the generative model must change
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Soccer's dc-v1 works because a soccer match is a LOW-COUNT event process: ~2.7
goals/match, goals are rare Poisson-ish arrivals, and the interesting markets
(O1.5, U3.5, BTTS, correct score) live in the first few integer cells — exactly
where the tau low-score correction earns its keep. The scoreline is modeled as a
product of two Poisson lambdas; probabilities come from a 11×11 score matrix.

Basketball breaks every one of those assumptions:
  - HIGH COUNT. An NBA team scores ~110-120 points across ~98-100 possessions.
    You are summing ~100 near-independent possession outcomes per team per game.
  - CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM APPLIES. A sum of ~100 possession values is
    approximately NORMAL. The team-score and (more usefully) the MARGIN
    distributions are well-described by a Gaussian, not a Poisson. This is the
    single most important modeling fact in basketball betting and it is why the
    whole sport is modeled on a CONTINUOUS margin, not a discrete score matrix.
  - MARGIN, NOT SCORELINE, IS THE NATURAL UNIT. Spread/ML are functions of the
    margin distribution; total is a function of the sum distribution. There is no
    "correct score" market worth pricing (the score-cell probabilities are
    diffuse and nobody books them seriously). A score matrix is the wrong object.
  - EMPIRICAL SD IS STABLE AND KNOWN. Game-to-game margin has a standard
    deviation of ~11-13 points (practitioner consensus ~12-12.5; regression on
    pace/efficiency explains only ~21% of MOV variance — the rest is irreducible
    game noise, which is precisely why the market can only be matched, not beaten)
    [thedatajocks; apbrmetrics; rstudio-pubs CBB normal-dist].

CONCLUSION: the CORE generative model is a PACE × EFFICIENCY point-differential
model whose output is a NORMAL (with a skew/t refinement, see 2d) distribution
over MARGIN and over TOTAL. Dixon-Coles is retired for the team model. (Poisson/
negative-binomial DOES return — but downstream, for player COUNTING stats in the
props model, §4, where counts are low again: a player's rebounds ARE a low-count
arrival process.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(2) THE CORE TEAM MODEL — pace × efficiency → margin+total distribution
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2a. THE IDENTITY (the whole model in one line)
    Points = Possessions × Points-Per-Possession.
    We predict the two sides separately, opponent-adjusted, then combine:

      Poss_game   = f(pace_home, pace_away)                 (a rate, shared)
      PPP_home    = g(ORtg_home, DRtg_away, context)        (offense vs defense)
      PPP_away    = g(ORtg_away, DRtg_home, context)
      Pts_home    = Poss_game × PPP_home
      Pts_away    = Poss_game × PPP_away
      MARGIN (mu_m) = Pts_home − Pts_away
      TOTAL  (mu_t) = Pts_home + Pts_away

    Ratings are expressed per-100-possessions (the standard unit) so pace and
    efficiency are cleanly separable — the KenPom/Pomeroy/Oliver decomposition.
    Dean Oliver's "Four Factors" (eFG%, TOV%, ORB%, FT rate) are the structural
    decomposition of PPP and give us interpretable, opponent-adjustable
    sub-ratings if/when we want them; v1 can operate on aggregate ORtg/DRtg.

2b. POSSESSIONS (pace) — a shared rate, jointly determined
    Possessions per game estimated from the box-score identity
      Poss ≈ FGA − ORB + TOV + 0.44 × FTA
    Each team has a pace tendency (possessions they impose). Because both teams
    play the SAME number of possessions (to within one), the game pace is well
    approximated by combining the two tendencies against league average:
      Poss_game ≈ pace_home × pace_away / pace_league   (the "log-ish" combine)
    Pace must ALSO be opponent-adjusted (a fast team drags a slow team up) and is
    itself predictable — it is one of the more stable, more forecastable
    quantities in the sport, and it is the lever the TOTAL market lives on.
    [thedatajocks Pace&Efficiency Pt1; nbastuffer].

2c. OPPONENT-ADJUSTED EFFICIENCY RATINGS (the core estimation problem)
    Raw ORtg/DRtg are strength-of-schedule contaminated. We need ADJUSTED ratings
    — points per 100 possessions a team would score/allow vs a league-average
    opponent on a neutral floor. Three candidate estimators, in ascending
    sophistication; we build (i) first and treat (ii)/(iii) as challengers under
    the same promotion gate (§7):

    (i) RIDGE-REGRESSION ADJUSTED EFFICIENCY (the v1 workhorse).
        Treat each game's offensive output (pts/100) as
          y = mu + off_i − def_j + home·HCA + context + eps
        and solve the whole season's system by RIDGE (L2) regression for every
        team's off_i and def_j simultaneously. The L2 penalty shrinks toward the
        league mean (a prior), which is what makes early-season and thin-sample
        (WNBA, college small-conference) ratings stable rather than wild. This is
        the linear, transparent analog of KenPom's adjusted efficiency and the
        college adjusted-efficiency betting models [college-hoops-data; Riordan
        Medium; sradjoker SoS-adjustment]. Time-decay weight each game
        (exp(-xi·days_ago)) EXACTLY like dc-v1's xi — recency matters more in a
        roster-churn sport, and we reuse safepicks' xi-tuning grid machinery.

    (ii) ELO / GLICKO POINT-DIFFERENTIAL RATING (a robust cross-check and cold-
        start prior). A margin-aware Elo (update on MOV, not just W/L, with
        diminishing returns on blowouts and a preseason regression toward a
        roster-adjusted mean) is cheap, streaming, and famously well-calibrated
        for team strength (FiveThirtyEight/Silver-style). It carries ratings
        ACROSS seasons through the info-poor early weeks better than a within-
        season regression, so it seeds the ridge prior in games 1-15. Kept as a
        permanent ensemble member, not discarded.

    (iii) RAPM / PLAYER-AGGREGATED RATINGS (the info-ceiling frontier and the
        bridge to the player model). Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus solves a
        possession-level ridge system over the 10 players on the floor to get each
        player's offensive/defensive per-100 impact with an informed prior
        (L-RAPM). Team rating = sum of projected on-court players' RAPM. This is
        the ONLY estimator that natively handles the thing that actually moves
        lines — WHO is playing — and it is the shared spine with §4's player
        model (DARKO/LEBRON/EPM/BPM are all RAPM-priored). It is also where the
        information ceiling bites: RAPM is only as good as our MINUTES/lineup
        projection, which is the private-information the market prices and we
        approximate. Build last; it is the differentiator AND the ceiling.
        [arxiv L-RAPM 2601.15000; John Chen RAPM; evanmiya BPR].

2d. FROM POINT ESTIMATES TO A DISTRIBUTION (the deliverable)
    The market/total markets need probabilities, so we wrap the two point
    estimates in a predictive distribution:

      MARGIN  ~ Normal(mu_m, sigma_m)      [skew-t refinement below]
      TOTAL   ~ Normal(mu_t, sigma_t)

    * sigma_m ≈ 11-13 pts is the dominant term and is EMPIRICAL, not fitted from
      our features (our features explain only ~21% of MOV variance — the residual
      IS sigma_m). We estimate it from historical residuals of OUR OWN model,
      per-league (NBA ~11.5-12.5; WNBA/college differ), and let it FLEX with
      context: sigma shrinks slightly in low-total games and for large mu (a
      known heteroskedasticity — blowout margins fan out), and widens with
      lineup/injury uncertainty (a genuinely valuable, honest signal — when we
      don't know the minutes, we WIDEN, we don't bluff a tight number).
    * sigma_t (total) is separately estimated; margin and total are only weakly
      correlated so they are modeled as two marginals, not one bivariate (we do
      NOT need the joint for the listed markets; if we ever price margin-total
      combos we add a Gaussian copula).
    * SKEW / FAT TAILS. Pure Normal is ~90% of the value. Two documented
      refinements, added only if they earn it under calibration (§6): (a) a
      slight fat tail (Student-t, ν≈8-10) better fits blowout/garbage-time and
      overtime mass; (b) the KEY POINTS problem — NBA margins cluster on
      integers and disproportionately on specific numbers, but far LESS than NFL
      (no 3/7 spike); a smoothed empirical or a mild discretization correction
      near whole-number spreads captures push probability honestly. These are the
      basketball analog of dc-v1's tau low-score correction: small, targeted
      density fixes where the money actually sits (the hook, the push).

2e. DERIVING THE MARKET PROBABILITIES (all four from the two marginals)
      Spread (home −s):   P(MARGIN > s)      = 1 − Φ((s − mu_m)/sigma_m)
      Moneyline (home):   P(MARGIN > 0)      = 1 − Φ((0 − mu_m)/sigma_m)
      Total over line L:  P(TOTAL > L)       = 1 − Φ((L − mu_t)/sigma_t)
      Team total (home):  home pts ~ Normal((mu_t+mu_m)/2, sigma_teamtotal)
                          → P(home pts > L) directly.
      Push mass on integer lines handled by the 2d discretization; alt-lines are
      just other L values off the SAME distribution — one distribution prices the
      entire ladder, which is exactly the property the props/devig engine wants.

2f. JUSTIFICATION VS ALTERNATIVES (why this architecture, not another)
    * vs POISSON SCORE-MATRIX (dc-v1 ported): wrong stochastic object — CLT says
      margin is Gaussian; a score matrix wastes effort on diffuse cells nobody
      books and mis-prices the continuous spread ladder. Rejected on first
      principles (§1).
    * vs A PURE ELO: Elo gives a strong margin mean but a fixed/implicit variance
      and NO native total, no team totals, no pace lever, no player bridge. We
      KEEP Elo as an ensemble member and cold-start prior, but it cannot be the
      whole model because the props product needs pace and player structure.
    * vs A PURE ML BLACK BOX (LightGBM on margin, like safepicks' stack):
      basketball's margin is so close to "opponent-adjusted-rating-diff + HCA +
      rest," a monotone structural model gets ~all the signal with full
      interpretability and stable extrapolation to thin leagues. We STILL train a
      gradient-boosted CHALLENGER (mirroring safepicks' stack-v2) on the residual
      — its job is to squeeze non-linear context (rest×travel interactions,
      pace-mismatch, specific injury cascades) — but as a residual/blend
      component under the promotion gate, never the un-inspectable primary. This
      is precisely safepicks' dc-v1-primary + stack-challenger + market-blend
      shape, re-instantiated on a Gaussian core.
    * NET: a structural pace×efficiency Gaussian model = interpretable, thin-
      league-robust, natively prices the whole ladder, and shares its spine (RAPM)
      with the player model. It is the right CORE; ML and market are blend layers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(3) FEATURES — and the free-derivable vs info-ceiling-blocked split
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The honest dividing line (the whole feasibility thesis in one table): features
we can DERIVE from free public data move us toward market-GRADE; the features
that would beat the market are INFORMATION we get late/noisily or not at all.

FREE-DERIVABLE (nba_api / box scores / schedule — we can be as good as anyone):
  - Adjusted ORtg / DRtg (ridge, §2c)          — the core signal.
  - Pace / possessions (box-score identity)    — stable, forecastable.
  - Home-court advantage (~2.0-2.7 pts NBA,     — declining historically; fit
    fit per-league; smaller than it was)          per-season, don't hardcode.
  - REST / SCHEDULE (fully known in advance):
      * Back-to-back (2nd night): defense sags ~+1.5-2.5 pts/100 allowed; a B2B
        team loses ~1.8 pts on the spread; rested home vs B2B covers >55%. Totals
        can dip (fatigue slows pace). These are ROBUST, publicly-known, and
        already largely in the closing line — we include them to MATCH the line,
        not to beat it. [nbastuffer; heatcheckhq; inpredictable].
      * 3-in-4 nights, 4-in-6, days-rest differential (relative-rest index),
        second-game-of-road-trip — all schedule-derivable, all small (1-3 pts).
  - TRAVEL / altitude (Denver), time-zone shift, road-trip length — derivable
    from schedule+geo; small but real, and cheap.
  - RECENT FORM — handled by the time-decay weights (xi) on the ratings, NOT as a
    naive "last-10" feature (which is mostly noise + variance-chasing; the
    feasibility memo's warning against confusing predictability with edge). We
    trust decayed opponent-adjusted ratings over hot-hand narratives.

INFO-CEILING-BLOCKED (this is why we can't beat the main line — be blunt):
  - MINUTES / ROTATION INTENT — the master variable for BOTH the team RAPM
    rollup and every player prop. Officially unknown until tip; load-management
    and blowout garbage-time are semi-random. We can PROJECT it (§4a) but the
    market prices the coach's actual plan, which we don't have.
  - LATE INJURY / LOAD-MANAGEMENT declarations — league-mandated reports help
    (NBA is far more transparent than soccer, feasibility §5) but the DECISIVE
    final bulletin lands 15-30 min pre-tip and swings the main line 4-5 pts
    within MINUTES. Transparency denies the insider an edge; it does not hand us
    one on the main line. Its ONLY exploitable residue is the slow second-order
    prop cascade onto teammates (the props door, §4d).
  - STARTING LINEUP CONFIRMATION, in-game adjustments, matchup-specific game
    plans, sharp money flow — all priced faster than we can act.

DESIGN CONSEQUENCE: the core model's sigma_m must WIDEN under lineup uncertainty
(2d) so that "we don't know who's playing" shows up as honest doubt, not false
confidence. The model that says "I'm 92% here" when a star is questionable is the
exact failure mode safepicks' band-bias metric was invented to catch.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(4) THE PLAYER MODEL FOR PROPS — the differentiator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the reason the project exists. It is a hierarchical, distributional
projection: MINUTES → USAGE/OPPORTUNITY → PER-STAT RATE → per-prop DISTRIBUTION.
We do not output a point estimate; we output a full distribution per stat so we
can price ANY line and any alt-line and devig against soft books/DFS.

4a. MINUTES PROJECTION (the ceiling on everything — and the hardest part)
    "Minutes is the ceiling on everything else": no counting stat can exceed the
    opportunity minutes provide [rotogrinders; nbastuffer]. Model a player's
    minutes as a DISTRIBUTION, not a number:
      - Baseline from decayed recent minutes (role), conditioned on games where
        the same teammates were active/inactive (lineup-state conditioning).
      - Blowout risk (garbage-time haircut) driven by the CORE model's margin
        distribution — favorites' starters have a left tail; this is a real
        linkage where the team model feeds the player model.
      - Foul-out / injury-exit hazard (thin tail).
      - Back-to-back / rest load-management haircut for veterans.
      - Output: a minutes distribution (e.g. a censored/truncated Normal or a
        small mixture) whose UNCERTAINTY is the dominant driver of prop variance.
    This is the info-ceiling made concrete: our minutes distribution is our
    biggest edge on neglected lines AND our biggest source of honest error.

4b. USAGE / OPPORTUNITY LAYER
    Given minutes, convert to opportunity:
      - USG% (share of team possessions a player ends via FGA, FTA-trip, TOV);
        starters 18-25%, primaries 27-32%, stars 33%+ [nba.com/stats; nbastats
        forbetting]. Usage is fairly stable per role and SHIFTS predictably when
        a teammate is out (the cascade, 4d).
      - Team possessions available = CORE model's Poss_game (the pace lever again
        links team→player).
      - Per-position/scheme rates for the non-usage stats: rebound chances (team
        & opponent ORB/DRB rates × minutes), assist opportunity (teammate FG made
        while on floor), 3PA rate.

4c. PER-STAT DISTRIBUTIONS (the right distribution per stat — this is the craft)
    Counting stats are LOW-COUNT again → discrete arrival models return:
      - POINTS: a COMPOUND distribution, not a single family. Points =
        2·(2P made) + 3·(3P made) + (FT made). Model FGA (≈ minutes×usage×pace)
        then makes as Binomial given attempts and a matchup-adjusted FG%/3P%,
        plus FT trips×FT%. Convolve → an approximately-Normal-but-lumpy points
        distribution (the 3-pointer adds discrete 3-point jumps and fat right
        tail). Points variance is dominated by SHOOTING luck — the least
        "modelable" of the big three, which is exactly why books price points
        MOST carefully and the neglected stats (below) are softer.
      - REBOUNDS: NEGATIVE BINOMIAL. Counts are overdispersed vs Poisson
        (variance > mean; VMR > ~1.3 for volatile roles) driven by role
        volatility [dumbmoneypicks VMR; squared2020 NB regression]. Rebounds are
        the MOST predictable counting stat — tightly tied to minutes, position,
        and opponent rebounding rate — the single best prop-modeling target.
      - ASSISTS: NEGATIVE BINOMIAL, mean = f(minutes, usage, teammate FG%). More
        predictable than points in EXPECTATION (opportunity-driven) but carries an
        irreducible teammate-shooting-luck term (a PG's assists swing on whether
        shooters hit) — so we model it but keep honest wide intervals.
      - THREES MADE: Binomial(3PA, 3P%) or Negative Binomial — but HIGH VARIANCE
        (make/miss luck dominates); modelable in mean, noisy in outcome.
      - BLOCKS / STEALS: Poisson/NB with LOW rate (0.5-1.5), so nearly ALL the
        distribution mass is shooting-luck-like noise; these are the least
        predictable-in-outcome (§5).
      - PRA and other COMBOS (P+R, P+A, R+A): SUM of correlated marginals. Do NOT
        just add point estimates — convolve the distributions WITH their
        correlation (minutes is the shared driver, so components are positively
        correlated; a big-minutes night lifts all three). A Gaussian copula on
        the per-stat marginals, correlation estimated per archetype, gives the
        combo distribution. Combos have LOWER relative variance than components
        (one category compensates another) — which is why P+R is a favored,
        more-calibratable target [hownbabettingwork; propellerpicks].

4d. THE INJURY CASCADE (the second-order edge the feasibility memo flagged)
    When a starter is ruled out, the MAIN line reprices in minutes (no edge), but
    the redistribution of his ~30 minutes and ~28% usage onto teammates is
    repriced SLOWLY and UNEVENLY across 200-400 prop lines. Our model handles
    this natively BECAUSE it is hierarchical: remove the player from the lineup
    state, re-solve minutes (his minutes flow to specific teammates by
    role/position), bump their usage, re-project. This is the one place a real
    model beats a lazy book rather than merely shopping it — and it is a direct,
    mechanical output of the minutes→usage→rate chain, not a bolt-on.

4e. WHAT THE PLAYER MODEL IS FOR (keep the feasibility frame front of mind)
    The player distributions feed the DEVIG/LINE-SHOP engine (bball-04, separate
    memo): sharp consensus (Pinnacle/Circa) → fair prob → flag soft-book/DFS
    drift. Our model is OPTIONAL on well-covered points lines (the market is
    sharp there) and DECISIVE on NEGLECTED lines (rebounds/assists/threes/bench-
    cascade, WNBA, college) where even the sharp consensus is thin — that thin-
    market slice is the only place modeling beats rather than shops the market.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(5) CALIBRATE vs NOISE — the honest market map (the basketball analog of soccer's
    cards/BTTS anti-predictive verdict)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Soccer's lesson: some markets are genuinely calibratable, others are efficient/
anti-predictive at the price and confidence there is a TRAP (cards went -0.8% →
-14.4% ROI as model confidence rose). The basketball map:

CAN CALIBRATE WELL (tight reliability, low band-ECE achievable; MARKET-GRADE — we
match the line, we do not beat it, and we say so):
  - Full-game SPREAD, MONEYLINE, TOTAL — the CLT-Gaussian core prices these
    cleanly and calibration is excellent. Expect market-grade, ~0 edge net of
    vig. Value = a trustworthy anchor + devig cross-check, not a bet signal.
  - TEAM TOTALS — same distribution, slightly wider; calibratable.
  - REBOUNDS props — the single best-calibrating player market (minutes+position
    +opponent-rate driven, NB-modelable). Prime target.
  - POINTS props and P+R / PRA combos — calibratable in expectation; combos
    especially because summing correlated stats shrinks relative variance. Points
    alone carries shooting variance but the LINE is modelable even if the outcome
    is noisy (calibration is about the probability, not certainty of outcome).
  - ASSISTS props for high-usage lead guards — calibratable mean, honest-wide
    intervals; secondary players' assists are noisier.
  - MINUTES / "to record a double-double" for entrenched stars — the stable end.

CANNOT CALIBRATE / NOISE / ANTI-PREDICTIVE (the cards/BTTS analogs — model
confidence here is the trap; we DISPLAY-ONLY or EXCLUDE, never bet our own edge):
  - BLOCKS and STEALS props — rare-event (rate 0.5-1.5), variance-dominated;
    books "struggle to price precisely" but so do WE — outcome is mostly luck.
    Devig/line-shop them if the market disagrees, but NEVER trust our point
    projection as an edge. Highest-noise counting stats.
  - THREES-MADE props — modelable mean, but make/miss luck dominates the outcome;
    treat like points-with-extra-variance, lean on devig not model conviction.
  - FIRST-BASKET / FIRST-TO-SCORE, exact-stat, "player to score in every
    quarter," race-to-N-points — near-pure noise / novelty; the basketball
    equivalent of exotic soccer corner/card derivatives. EXCLUDE from any model-
    edge claim.
  - QUARTER / HALF sub-totals and sub-margins — thinner samples, more variance,
    and the lineup/rotation noise is proportionally larger; calibratable in
    principle but low priority and easy to over-trust. Display-only until proven.
  - LIVE / IN-GAME everything — out of scope; a different (and adversarial) game.

THE RULE (ported from safepicks' "sot display-only," "cards marginal" doctrine):
a market enters the SAFE/edge pool ONLY after it clears the calibration gate on a
held-out window (§6). Blocks/steals/threes/first-basket are display-or-devig-only
by default. We do not repeat the soccer cards mistake of letting a confident-
looking-but-anti-predictive market into the trusted pool.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(6) CALIBRATION — ported wholesale from safepicks, extended for distributions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We inherit safepicks' calibration stack verbatim in PHILOSOPHY and largely in
code shape (calibration.py, metrics.py), extended for the continuous-distribution
outputs basketball adds.

6a. PROBABILITY CALIBRATION (binary markets: each side of spread/ML/total/prop)
    Same three-candidate, held-out-selected machinery as safepicks:
      - IdentityCalibrator (no-op; calibration must never make it worse),
        IsotonicCalibrator (step function, breakpoints persisted),
        BetaCalibrator (Kull et al. logistic on log-odds features).
      - Fit on the chronological first ~70% of walk-forward predictions, judge on
        the held-out ~30%; pick the winner per (league, market/line, side).
      - THE gate metric is BAND-ECE / BAND-BIAS in the operating band [0.80,0.98)
        with the signed-bias scalar that "directly corrupts EV" — safepicks'
        exact insight that a model can look globally calibrated and still be 3pts
        optimistic at 0.90, which at prop odds is the whole margin. We keep the
        equal-mass ECE estimator (band samples are small; equal-width bins have a
        noise floor above the 0.02 gate).
      - Per-line calibration for props EXACTLY like totals_calibration.py fits
        per-(line,side) isotonic for OU15/25/35: rebounds-over-7.5 is a different
        calibration cell from rebounds-over-9.5. Artifact-gated, identity until
        fit, gitignored + regenerated.

6b. DISTRIBUTIONAL CALIBRATION (new for basketball — the Gaussian core & player
    distributions must be calibrated AS DISTRIBUTIONS, not just at one line)
      - PIT / PROBABILITY-INTEGRAL-TRANSFORM histograms: for each realized margin/
        total/stat, compute F_pred(actual); if the predictive distribution is
        honest, PITs are Uniform(0,1). A U-shaped PIT = overconfident (sigma too
        small — the exact failure the feasibility memo warns about); a hump =
        underconfident. This is the CONTINUOUS analog of a reliability curve and
        it directly validates our sigma choices in §2d/§4a.
      - CRPS (Continuous Ranked Probability Score) as the primary distributional
        scoring rule — the proper-scoring generalization of Brier to a full
        predictive distribution; used to compare candidate sigma models and skew/
        t refinements, and to compare us vs the market-implied distribution.
      - A calibrated sigma is the whole ballgame for props: get the MEAN right and
        the WIDTH wrong and every over/under is mispriced. PIT/CRPS gate every
        refinement in §2d and §4.

6c. WHAT WE REPORT (the safepicks metric suite, per market, per league):
      Brier, full-range ECE, band-ECE, band-bias, log-loss, reliability curve —
      PLUS CRPS and PIT for the distributional outputs — PLUS the market-relative
      number (below). Calibration artifacts are versioned JSON, gitignored,
      regenerated by a `calibrate_*` command, loaded process-cached with mtime
      invalidation (totals_calibration.py pattern).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(7) MODEL VERSIONING + BACKTEST / FORWARD-VALIDATION PLAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7a. VERSIONING (mirror safepicks' ModelVersion + champion/challenger)
    - Named ModelVersion rows: core-v1 (ridge pace×efficiency Gaussian),
      elo-v1 (ensemble member), stack-v1 (gbm residual challenger), blend-v1
      (core + market prior), player-v1 (minutes→usage→rate). Each carries algo,
      feature_set_version, train_window, artifact_uri, metrics, is_active — the
      exact safepicks schema. is_active flips ONLY on human promotion review.
    - CHAMPION/CHALLENGER PROMOTION GATE (safepicks ml/promotion.py, ported):
      a challenger promotes ONLY when it beats the champion on (a) held-out band-
      bias / band-ECE (and CRPS for distributions), (b) per-market MBI, and
      (c) flat-stake yield / paper-CLV, over >= 2 seasons across >= 3 leagues.
      No single-metric promotions; no promotion on the development window alone.

7b. WALK-FORWARD BACKTEST (the only honest backtest — safepicks discipline)
    - Seasonal walk-forward folds: fold i fits STRICTLY on games before cutoff_i
      and predicts forward (training.walk_forward_folds shape). Ratings, sigma,
      calibrators, blend weights — everything is fit only on the past. A burn-in
      minimum (dc-v1 uses 130 matches; basketball: ~10-15 games/team early-season,
      leaning on the Elo prior through burn-in) before the first real prediction.
    - Recency weighting (exp decay, xi) inside every fold, tuned per league on
      out-of-sample log-loss/CRPS on EARLY seasons only (tune_dc pattern).

7c. THE LEAKAGE BOUNDARY (ADR-006 doctrine, non-negotiable)
    - CLOSING odds are the BENCHMARK, never an input. Any pre-closing market
      prior used in blend-v1 is is_closing=False ONLY, enforced twice (ORM filter
      + a LeakageError raise on any closing flag) — the belt-and-braces pattern
      from blend.py. B0/closing is the yardstick we measure against, full stop.
    - MINUTES/lineup features must use only information available pre-tip at the
      prediction timestamp — no post-hoc actual-minutes leakage into training
      targets vs features. (Actual minutes is the TARGET for the minutes model;
      it must never appear as a same-game FEATURE for the stat models.)

7d. THE SEALED HOLDOUT (repeated-tuning-overfits doctrine)
    - Designate a development window and SEAL a forward window that is NEVER
      evaluated or reported until final validation — the exact "Q1 is dev,
      Apr-Jun stays sealed, do not even look" rule. For a new build: use prior
      seasons as walk-forward dev, seal the most recent full season (and the live
      forward feed) as the untouched holdout for any candidate that wins on dev.

7e. FORWARD PAPER-VALIDATION (the load-bearing one, given thin prop history)
    - Prop odds history only goes back ~2023 (feasibility §6) — too thin for a
      soccer-grade "Last Dance" deep backtest. So the PRIMARY validation is
      FORWARD PAPER-TRADING: log every prediction with a timestamp, capture the
      line we would have taken and the eventual CLOSING line, and score:
        * CLV (closing-line value) — did we beat the number that later closed?
          The single most predictive proxy for long-run edge.
        * Realized Brier/ECE/band-bias/CRPS on settled results, rolling.
        * Market-Beating-Index (MBI = market log-loss − model log-loss) per market
          — positive means we beat the closing line (expected ~0/negative on main
          markets = market-grade confirmation; the signal to watch is POSITIVE MBI
          on neglected props/soft venues, the live edge).
      Reuse safepicks' paper_clv logger pattern wholesale.
    - The forward log doubles as the promotion evidence (7a-c) and the honesty
      ledger: it will, if the feasibility memo is right, SHOW core-v1 going
      market-grade on main lines and the player model earning its keep only on
      neglected/soft prop lines. That is the expected, honest result — and the
      validation plan is designed to reveal it, not hide it.

(8) BUILD ORDER (dependency-first, calibration-gated at each step)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Possessions/pace estimator + ridge adjusted ORtg/DRtg (core-v1 mean).
  2. Elo margin model (cold-start prior + ensemble member).
  3. sigma_m / sigma_t estimation + Normal wrap → spread/ML/total/team-total
     probabilities; PIT/CRPS calibration gate. (Market-grade anchor done.)
  4. Probability calibrators (isotonic/beta/identity, band-ECE selection) +
     per-line calibration scaffolding. Skew/t refinement iff it clears CRPS.
  5. Minutes distribution model (the hard, info-ceiling piece).
  6. Usage/opportunity + per-stat NB/compound distributions (rebounds first —
     best-calibrating — then points, assists, threes; combos via copula).
  7. Injury-cascade lineup-state re-solve.
  8. gbm residual challenger (stack-v1) + market blend (blend-v1), both under the
     promotion gate.
  9. Forward paper-CLV logger + walk-forward backtest harness + seal the holdout.
  Each stage ships ONLY after clearing its calibration gate; nothing enters the
  trusted/edge pool on structural plausibility alone (the §5 rule).
