Soccer Prediction Platform · Django + PostgreSQL + Celery + ML

Master Build Plan

An AI/ML soccer prediction system engineered around what actually makes money: calibrated probabilities, value detection vs bookmaker odds, and strict risk management — not accuracy claims. Two products: a SAFE picks feed (85%+ hit-rate derived markets) and a VALUE picks feed (+EV selections staked by quarter-Kelly).

Reality Check — What "Nearing 100%" Actually Means

Hard truth first, because the whole architecture depends on it: no model predicts soccer at near-100%. Soccer is low-scoring and high-variance; the theoretical 1X2 ceiling is ~75–80% even with perfect information. Bookmakers' own models — the best-funded in the world — are only ~55–58% accurate and they still profit, because profit does not come from accuracy. It comes from three things:

PILLAR 1

🎯Calibration

When your model says 60%, it happens 60% of the time. Calibration — not accuracy — is what makes a probability worth money.

PILLAR 2

💰Value

Bet only when your probability exceeds the bookmaker's implied probability — positive expected value (+EV). No edge, no bet.

PILLAR 3

🛡️Risk Control

Stake so variance cannot bankrupt you: fractional Kelly, exposure caps, drawdown limits, circuit breakers.

The reframed goal: "Maximize risk-adjusted return by producing better-calibrated probabilities than the market on selected markets, and betting only where a statistically verified edge exists."

📈Where near-high accuracy IS achievable — and how we exploit it

  • Heavy favorites in cup mismatches (85–92% win prob — but odds ~1.05, so no value; the model must know this).
  • Derived low-risk markets: Double Chance (1X/X2), Over 0.5 goals, Draw No Bet, Asian Handicap heavy lines — these routinely price at 80–95% probability.
  • Accuracy on such markets can legitimately reach 85–93% hit rate — the app's "safe picks" feed. Profitability still requires value filtering, because a high hit-rate at bad odds loses money.

🛡️Product A — SAFE PICKS 85%+ hit rate

High-probability, low-odds selections (DC, DNB, O0.5, AH heavy lines) with hit rates targeting 85%+.

📊Product B — VALUE PICKS 3–8% ROI/bet

+EV selections at any odds level, ranked by edge and Kelly stake, targeting long-run ROI of 3–8% per bet — realistic elite performance.

0.5

Predictability vs Beatability NEW v2.0

Field observation that motivated this section: lower leagues often feel more predictable than top leagues. Decompose that feeling carefully — two different things get called "predictable", and they point in opposite directions.

🔬A · Raw Outcome Predictability top leagues win

Measured as model accuracy / log-loss. Lower divisions have more parity (no Bayern/Celtic-style dominance), higher draw rates, lower scoring, heavier squad churn, and more motivation noise — a Dixon-Coles or Elo model shows worse accuracy and calibration there, not better.

🥊B · Market Beatability low leagues win

Measured as edge vs the bookmaker — and this is what the feeling really is. The EPL closing line is the sharpest market in sports: disagree with it and you are usually wrong. A lower-league line is set by a junior model, moves slowly, and carries fat margins: disagree with it and you are right far more often. The predictability did not go up; the competition got worse.

📐Measurement — the Market Beatability Index (MBI)

Computed per league (and per league-market cell) by the backtester (§9):

(a) model log-loss  ·  (b) de-vigged closing-line log-loss  ·  MBI = (b) − (a) (a) = raw predictability · (b) = market sharpness · MBI = the gap = harvestable edge

The pick engine follows the MBI, not raw accuracy. Expect the MBI to grow down the pyramid even while (a) worsens — if data quality holds.

⚠️Cautions — why "bet the lowest leagues" is still wrong

  • Thin data ⇒ model confidence there is miscalibrated in ways a results-only backtest cannot reveal (unverifiable lineups and team news).
  • Integrity risk concentrates in lower divisions; fixing can even manufacture short streaks of eerie "predictability" before burning the bankroll. Auto-blacklist rules in [ODDS §8] apply.
  • Books cap stakes low and limit winners fast in minor leagues — the edge is real but hard to scale.
Operational conclusion: the sweet spot stays mid-tier European leagues [ODDS §5 trade-off triangle] — decent data, honest competitions, meaningfully softer markets than the Big-5.

In prose: a single Django project owns ten apps, one per concern, from raw ingestion through features, predictions, odds, value detection, backtesting and portfolio tracking to the dashboard and REST API. Everything persists to one PostgreSQL instance; Celery workers driven by Celery Beat perform all recurring jobs; model training happens offline in a separate pipeline that publishes versioned artifacts the Django service layer loads for inference.

🗄️PostgreSQL = single source of truth

Raw data, features, predictions, odds snapshots, bets, results — everything versioned.

⏰Celery Beat drives all recurring work

No cron sprawl — one schedule, one audit trail.

🧠Training offline, inference online

ML training runs in scripts/CI; the Django service layer loads pinned model artifacts. Never train in a request.

🧾Every prediction is auditable

Each prediction row stores model version, feature snapshot hash, probability vector, and the odds available at prediction time — honest backtesting and auditing.

03

Data Sources — The Moat

Model quality is capped by data quality. Plan for three budget tiers.

TIER 0 · Free MVP bootstrap

  • football-data.co.uk — 25+ seasons CSV, results + closing odds (Bet365, Pinnacle, market max/avg) for ~25 leagues. THE backtesting goldmine. League CSV codes in [ODDS §6].
  • football-data.org API — fixtures, results, standings (free tier).
  • Understat / FBref scraping — xG, shot data (respect robots.txt/ToS; FBref data is via StatsBomb, check terms).
  • OpenLigaDB, API mirrors — supplemental.

TIER 1 · Cheap Paid production MVP

  • API-Football (api-sports.io) — fixtures, lineups, injuries, live scores, odds from 20+ books, ~$25–60/mo tiers.
  • The Odds API — clean multi-bookmaker odds feed, good for line shopping and closing-line capture.

TIER 2 · Serious Edge later

  • StatsBomb / Opta / Sportmonks — event-level data, player-level xG chains, pressure data. Expensive; only when ROI justifies.
  • Pinnacle API (via broker) — sharpest closing lines = best truth signal for calibration benchmarking.

📥Ingestion rules

  • Store raw payloads (JSONB) + parsed normalized rows. Raw is immutable; parsers can re-run when bugs are found.
  • Every entity gets a canonical ID + alias table. Cross-source name matching is the #1 practical pain — build TeamAlias + fuzzy matcher + manual review queue on day one.
  • Idempotent sync jobs keyed on (source, external_id).

🧪Data-completeness scoring v2.0

  • Every match gets a completeness score in [0,1] at feature-build time: history depth, odds coverage (books × markets), xG / lineup / injury-report availability.
  • Stored on the feature snapshot; the pick engine refuses to publish below a per-tier threshold (SAFE stricter than VALUE).
  • Main defense for the §0.5 thin-data caution: soft-market leagues only become bettable when their data actually supports it.

🚦API budget & rate limits v2.0

  • Every client wraps its source with: daily request budget, token-bucket rate limiter, exponential backoff + jitter, Redis response cache with per-endpoint TTL, and a job_run log entry per sync.
  • Polling degrades gracefully when budget runs low: final-2h polling is protected first, T-7d discovery sacrificed first.
  • Never scrape behind auth or against ToS; prefer paid tiers when a source becomes load-bearing — flaky free data poisons everything.

Feature Engineering — The Real Differentiator

All features computed "as of" kickoff-minus-X, never after. Feature sets are versioned (feature_set_version) so models are reproducible.

💪5.1 · Strength ratings

  • Elo (base + home-advantage term + goal-diff multiplier), K tuned per league; separate home/away Elo optionally.
  • Poisson attack/defense strengths (Dixon-Coles with time decay ~0.0065/day half-life ≈ backtested per league).
  • Optional: Glicko-2 (uncertainty-aware), pi-ratings.

📈5.2 · Form & rolling windows

5/10/20-match, exponentially weighted:

  • Points per game, goals for/against, xG for/against, shots (on target), corners, clean-sheet rate, BTTS rate, over-2.5 rate, first-half goal rate.
  • Home-only / away-only splits of all of the above.
  • Rest days, matches in last 14/21 days (fatigue/congestion), travel distance (Phase 3), competition context (league vs cup).

🤝5.3 · Head-to-head

H2H record last N meetings with time decay; style matchup features (possession-vs-counter proxies from shot/possession stats).

🧑‍🤝‍🧑5.4 · Squad signals Phase 2+

  • Starting XI strength delta vs season average (needs lineups ~1h before kickoff — enables a late "lineup-confirmed" re-prediction).
  • Key-player availability index (minutes-weighted), keeper change flag.

💹5.5 · Market features — the market is a feature

  • Opening odds implied probs (de-vig), current odds, line movement velocity, sharp-book (Pinnacle) vs soft-book divergence.
  • Caution: for VALUE detection, train a market-free model AND a market-aware model. The market-aware model is the accuracy champion; the market-free model finds disagreements = value candidates.

🌍5.6 · Context

League position, points-from-safety/title (motivation proxy), season stage, derby flag, weather (Phase 3), referee tendencies (cards/penalties markets, Phase 3), manager change recency.

🧪5.7 · Data-quality features v2.0

The completeness score (§3) itself enters the feature vector — the model learns how much to trust thin-data fixtures — and doubles as the pick-engine gate (§8). League format flags (playoff-split stage for AUT/BEL, dead-rubber flag) also live here.

De-vig method: multiplicative (proportional) for MVP, upgrade to Shin's method (accounts for favourite–longshot bias) — it measurably improves implied-probability truth. Library: shin (PyPI) or implement — a closed-form solution exists.
06

Modeling Strategy — Layered, Market by Market

6.1 · Baselines — build FIRST; they beat naive ML surprisingly often

B0 Market baseline

De-vigged closing odds AS the prediction. Any model must beat this on log-loss to claim an edge — most don't. The honesty benchmark that keeps the project real.

B1 Poisson GLM

goals_home ~ attack_h × defense_a × home_adv; derive the full scoreline matrix → every market's probability (1X2, O/U, BTTS, DC, DNB, AH, correct score) from ONE model.

B2 Dixon-Coles

B1 + low-score dependency correction + time decay. The 1997 model is still the workhorse of professional soccer modeling. Implement carefully; validate against published results.

6.2 · ML layer

M1 Gradient boosting

LightGBM/XGBoost multiclass 1X2 on the full feature set; monotonic constraints on rating-diff features.

M2 Per-market binary GBMs

Separate models for Over 2.5, BTTS, DNB… — often better calibrated than deriving from 1X2.

M3 Bivariate Poisson

Bivariate Poisson / negative binomial goals regression (statsmodels), blended with M1.

🏗️6.3 · Ensembling & calibration — where the money is

  • Stack: logistic meta-learner over {B2, M1, M3, market prob}.
  • Calibrate every final output: isotonic regression (large data) or beta calibration (small data), fit on a held-out time slice.

Metrics that matter, in order:

  1. Log-loss / Brier vs market baseline B0
  2. Expected Calibration Error (ECE), reliability diagrams
  3. Backtested ROI & max drawdown with realistic staking
  4. CLV — do our picks beat the close? Positive CLV is the single best predictor of long-run profit.

Plain "accuracy" is reported but never used for model selection.

🧯6.4 · Training protocol — leakage is the #1 killer

  • Strictly time-ordered walk-forward splits: train [t0,t1) → validate [t1,t2) → roll forward. Never random K-fold.
  • Per-league models where data allows; pooled model with league embeddings/one-hots for small leagues.
  • Retrain cadence: weekly full retrain; ratings (Elo/DC) update daily.
  • Hyperparameter search: Optuna, budgeted, on walk-forward CV only.
  • Every run logged to MLflow: params, metrics, feature importances, calibration plots, artifact.

🤖6.5 · Deep learning Phase 6, optional

Sequence models (transformer/GRU over last-N match event vectors), player-level graph features — only after the GBM edge is proven. Realistically adds 1–2% log-loss at 10× complexity; do not start here.

🧩6.6 · League-market cell specialization v2.0

  • The unit of edge is the (league, market) cell, not the league or the model: an Eredivisie-Overs specialist and a Serie B-Unders specialist will each beat one global everything-model [ODDS §10].
  • Benchmark every cell against B0 to compute its MBI (§0.5). Cells are enabled/disabled in market_rule by backtest evidence, not intuition.
  • Draws get no cell until a Phase 6 specialist model exists [ODDS §2].
07

Odds Engine & "Best Odds" Selection

📡Core engine

  • Polling schedule: every 6h at T-7d, hourly at T-24h, every 5–10 min in the final 2h (rate-limit aware).
  • Line shopping: keep the BEST decimal odds across bookmakers per (match, market, selection) — alone worth 1–3% ROI, the cheapest real edge in the whole system.
  • Closing-line capture (is_closing=TRUE) for every market — needed for CLV computation and model benchmarking.
  • De-vig every book's prices (Shin) → implied prob per book; market consensus prob weighted toward sharp books.
  • Steam / sharp-divergence detection — as features and as alerts.

⏱️Price-capture timing v2.0 · [ODDS §1C]

  • Model-liked favorites: capture EARLY (T-3d to T-24h) before public money shortens them; popular overs/big-club prices drift the other way. Backtest "price at T-24h vs closing" per market and store the optimal capture window on the market_rule row.
  • Steam-lag route: when a sharp book moves >4–5% and a soft book hasn't followed, the stale soft price is the cheapest edge available — surface it as an alert within minutes, not on the next hourly sync.
  • Routing: soft books for value capture, sharps/exchanges for volume and account longevity (limits_winners flag drives this).
08

Value Engine, Risk Management & Pick Generation

🔍8.1 · Value detection

edge = p_model_calibrated − p_market_devig  ·  EV = p_model × (odds_best − 1) − (1 − p_model)

Publish a VALUE pick only when all of:

  • edge ≥ threshold (start 3–5%, tuned by backtest, per market — seed values in [ODDS §3])
  • p_model within the model's proven-calibration range (no extrapolation)
  • odds within the market_rule window; global ceiling 7.0 — hard filter, no overrides [ODDS §2]
  • data-completeness score for the match ≥ threshold (§3)
  • league whitelisted: latest LPI ≥ 60 in league_metric ([ODDS §5]; 45–59 = training-data only)
  • model_version is the pinned production version

🛡️8.2 · SAFE PICKS tier

  • Candidate markets: Double Chance, Draw No Bet, Over 0.5/1.5, Asian Handicap heavy lines, "favorite to advance".
  • Selection rule: p_model ≥ 0.85 AND edge ≥ 1.5% AND odds ≥ 1.10.
  • Hit-rate target 85–92% — with the explicit warning that ROI per bet is small and one loss wipes several wins. Kelly handles this.

⚖️8.3 · Staking — non-negotiable defaults

  • Quarter-Kelly: stake = 0.25 × bankroll × (p·odds − 1)/(odds − 1), floored at 0, capped at 2% of bankroll.
  • Portfolio caps: max 10% open at once; 4% per match; 15% per league per day; correlated-pick detection (same match = one exposure).
  • Circuit breakers: pause auto-picks if 20-bet rolling CLV < 0, drawdown > 15%, or live ECE degrades 2× from backtest.
  • All parameters live in DB (risk_profile), editable in admin.

🔁8.4 · Pick lifecycle

generated→ published (dashboard/API/notification)→ odds re-checked at publish→ tracked→ settled by results job→ P&L · CLV · hit-rate → daily report
8.5 · Thin-week rule (v2.0): the system's default answer in a thin week is "fewer picks", never "lower thresholds". No calendar pressure, no quota. An empty pick list is a correct output. [ODDS §9]
09

Backtesting Framework — Build Before Believing Anything

🧮Engine & protocol

  • Vectorized pandas engine over historical matches + odds (football-data.co.uk closing odds are sufficient for MVP).
  • Walk-forward: for each season S — train on all data < S (with decay), predict S, apply the FULL pick-generation + staking pipeline, settle, compound bankroll.

Simulate honestly:

  • Odds available at prediction time (or closing as conservative proxy) minus a 1–2% slippage haircut.
  • Void/postponed handling included.
  • Never let features peek past kickoff — assert as-of timestamps.

📤Outputs & promotion rule

  • Equity curve, ROI/bet, yield, max drawdown, hit rate per market/league/odds-band, CLV distribution, calibration plots, p-value of profit vs luck (bootstrap resampling of bet outcomes).
  • Promotion rule: a model/config ships ONLY if it beats the current champion on walk-forward log-loss AND backtested risk-adjusted return over ≥ 2 full seasons across ≥ 3 leagues.

🗺️Per-league decomposition report v2.0 — writes league_metric rows

For every league with ≥ 2 backtested seasons, report side by side:

MetricMeaning
(a)model log-lossraw predictability
(b)closing-line log-lossmarket sharpness
(c)MBI = (b) − (a)harvestable edge (§0.5)

…plus draw rate, O2.5 rate, sample size, and per-cell yield. Ranked by MBI, this table IS the league strategy: it settles the "low leagues feel more predictable" question with evidence, feeds the LPI review, and flips market_rule cells on/off. Expect Big-5 cells to show good (a) but near-zero (c); expect soft-market league cells to show worse (a) but positive (c) — bet (c), not (a).

Honest Success Criteria — What "Best" Looks Like

🎯Model

  • Log-loss within 1–2% of (or better than) the de-vigged closing line
  • Live ECE < 0.03
  • SAFE tier hit rate ≥ 85% over 200+ picks

💵Economics

  • Positive CLV on ≥ 55% of picks
  • Backtested + paper-traded yield of 3–8% per bet on VALUE tier
  • Max drawdown < 20% at quarter-Kelly

🗺️League strategy v2.0

  • Every enabled (league, market) cell justified by a positive-MBI, positive-yield backtest over ≥ 2 seasons
  • No cell enabled on intuition
  • The /leagues page can explain WHY any league is in or out

🛠️Engineering

  • Full audit trail from any pick back to raw data, odds snapshot, feature snapshot, model version
  • Zero-leakage verified by tests
  • Retrain-to-production is one reviewed click
Discipline: the system refuses to bet when it has no edge. Most days the best pick list is SHORT. That restraint — not accuracy — is what separates profitable systems from losing ones.

Failure Modes & Anti-Patterns NEW v2.0

How these systems actually die — each with its built-in defense.

01Data leakage

The #1 killer. A feature quietly computed from post-kickoff data makes backtests spectacular and live betting ruinous. Defense: frozen feature snapshots, as-of assertions in tests (§10.5), suspicion of ANY too-good result.

02Backtest overfitting

Tuning thresholds until history looks great. One season, one league, 200 bets = curve-fitting, not evidence. Defense: walk-forward only, ≥2 seasons × ≥3 leagues promotion rule, bootstrap p-value on profit, out-of-sample holdout seasons.

03Odds availability bias

Backtesting on best-of-market odds you could never have actually taken. Defense: slippage haircut (§9), closing-line-as-proxy conservatism, live paper-trade comparison.

04Ignoring negative CLV

Bankroll up, CLV down = running on luck. The bankroll lies for months; CLV tells the truth in weeks. Defense: CLV circuit breaker (§8.3) trumps the equity curve.

05Chasing the tail

Model shows an 8% edge on a 9.0 longshot — almost always model miscalibration in the tail. Defense: 7.0 hard ceiling, calibration-range gate (§8.1).

06Quota pressure

Publishing picks because subscribers/users expect picks today. Defense: thin-week rule (§8.5); an empty list is a feature, not a bug.

07Soft-data mirage

A "predictable" low league that is really a thin-data artifact — or worse, a fixed one (§0.5). Defense: completeness gate, integrity auto-blacklist, MBI computed only on adequate sample sizes.

08Staking drift

Manually overriding Kelly after wins ("we're hot") or doubling after losses (Martingale instinct). Defense: stakes are computed, capped, and logged; manual bets recorded via API are flagged separately in P&L.

09Silent model rot

League regime changes (dominant era ends, format changes) degrade a model that keeps predicting yesterday's league. Defense: quarterly LPI/MBI review (§10.2), live-vs-backtest ECE monitor, champion/challenger always running.

10Infra debt at the worst time

Odds polling dies silently on matchday; picks publish from stale prices. Defense: job_run audit rows, Sentry alerts on missed beats, publish-time odds re-check.

Glossary NEW v2.0

1X2
Match result market: Home win (1), Draw (X), Away win (2).
AH
Asian Handicap — goal-start lines (incl. quarter lines); heavy favorite lines are a SAFE-tier staple.
BTTS
Both Teams To Score (Yes/No).
CLV
Closing Line Value — the % by which your taken odds beat the closing odds. The best long-run profit predictor.
DC
Double Chance (1X or X2) — sells draw risk cheaply.
De-vig
Removing the bookmaker margin from odds to recover the market's true implied probabilities (Shin's method here).
DNB
Draw No Bet — stake refunded on a draw.
ECE
Expected Calibration Error — average gap between claimed probability and observed frequency.
Edge
p_model_calibrated − p_market_devig for a selection.
EV
Expected Value per unit staked: p×(odds−1) − (1−p). Positive EV = value bet.
Kelly
Bankroll-fraction staking formula maximizing log growth; we use quarter-Kelly (25%) capped at 2% of bankroll.
LPI
League Predictability Index — 0–100 weighted league score (imbalance, draws, stability, churn, data, softness, integrity). Defined in [ODDS §5]; ≥ 60 = bettable.
MBI
Market Beatability Index — closing-line log-loss minus model log-loss, per league or (league, market) cell. Positive = harvestable edge (§0.5). The pick engine follows MBI, not raw accuracy.
O/U
Over/Under total-goals markets (0.5 / 1.5 / 2.5 lines).
Steam move
Sudden sharp-book line move from informed money; lagging soft-book prices become briefly exploitable.
Vig / Margin
The bookmaker's built-in profit: implied probabilities summing above 100%.
xG
Expected Goals — shot-quality-based scoring model; the strongest publicly available team-strength signal.
