--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. REALITY CHECK — WHAT "NEARING 100%" ACTUALLY MEANS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hard truth first, because the whole architecture depends on it:

  * No model predicts soccer match outcomes at near-100% accuracy. Soccer is
    low-scoring and high-variance; the theoretical ceiling for 1X2 (home/draw/
    away) accuracy is estimated around 75-80% even with perfect information.
    Bookmakers' own models — the best-funded in the world — are only ~55-58%
    accurate on 1X2 and they STILL profit, because profit does not come from
    accuracy. It comes from:

      1. CALIBRATION  — when your model says 60%, it happens 60% of the time.
      2. VALUE        — betting only when YOUR probability > the bookmaker's
                        implied probability (positive expected value, +EV).
      3. RISK CONTROL — staking so that variance cannot bankrupt you
                        (fractional Kelly, exposure caps, drawdown limits).

  * Therefore the goal of this application is reframed as:
      "Maximize risk-adjusted return by producing better-calibrated
       probabilities than the market on selected markets, and betting
       only where a statistically verified edge exists."

  * Where near-high accuracy IS achievable and we exploit it:
      - Heavy favorites in cup mismatches (85-92% win prob — but odds ~1.05,
        so no value; the model must know this).
      - Derived low-risk markets: Double Chance (1X / X2), Over 0.5 goals,
        Draw No Bet, Asian Handicap with heavy lines — these routinely price
        at 80-95% probability. The system will surface these as the
        "low-risk / high-probability" tier the user asked for.
      - Accuracy on such markets can legitimately reach 85-93% hit rate —
        the app's "safe picks" feed. Profitability still requires value
        filtering because high hit-rate at bad odds loses money.

  Design consequence: the system has TWO user-facing products:
      A) SAFE PICKS  — high-probability, low-odds selections (DC, DNB, O0.5,
         AH heavy lines) with hit rates targeting 85%+.
      B) VALUE PICKS — +EV selections at any odds level, ranked by edge and
         Kelly stake, targeting long-run ROI of 3-8% per bet (realistic elite
         performance).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.5 PREDICTABILITY vs BEATABILITY — TWO DIFFERENT TARGETS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Field observation that motivated this section: lower leagues often FEEL
  more predictable than top leagues. Decompose that feeling carefully,
  because two different things get called "predictable" and they point in
  opposite directions:

  A) RAW OUTCOME PREDICTABILITY (model accuracy / log-loss):
     Top leagues usually WIN here. Lower divisions have more parity (no
     Bayern/Celtic-style dominance), higher draw rates, lower scoring,
     heavier squad churn, and more motivation noise — a Dixon-Coles or Elo
     model shows WORSE accuracy and calibration there, not better.

  B) MARKET BEATABILITY (edge vs the bookmaker):
     Lower leagues usually WIN here, and this is what the feeling really
     is. The EPL closing line is the output of the sharpest market in
     sports — when you disagree with it, you are usually the one who is
     wrong. A lower-league line is set by a junior model with little
     attention, moves slowly, and carries fat margins — when you disagree
     with THAT line, you are right far more often. The predictability did
     not go up; the competition got worse.

  MEASUREMENT — the Market Beatability Index (MBI), computed per league
  by the backtester (§9):
      (a) model log-loss                    — raw predictability
      (b) de-vigged closing-line log-loss   — market sharpness
      MBI = (b) - (a)                        — the gap = harvestable edge
  The pick engine should follow the MBI, not raw accuracy. Expect the MBI
  to grow down the pyramid even while (a) worsens — IF data quality holds.

  CAUTIONS (why "bet the lowest leagues" is still wrong):
   * Thin data => model confidence there is miscalibrated in ways a
     results-only backtest cannot reveal (unverifiable lineups/news).
   * Integrity risk concentrates in lower divisions; fixing can even
     manufacture short streaks of eerie "predictability" before burning
     the bankroll. Auto-blacklist rules in [ODDS §8] apply.
   * Books cap stakes low and limit winners fast in minor leagues — the
     edge is real but hard to scale.
  => The operational sweet spot stays mid-tier European leagues
     [ODDS §5 trade-off triangle]: decent data, honest competitions,
     meaningfully softer markets than the Big-5.

  |  ticks)       |   |  pick publishing)|   +----------------------+

  In prose: a single Django project owns ten apps, one per concern, from
  raw ingestion through features, predictions, odds, value detection,
  backtesting and portfolio tracking to the dashboard and REST API.
  Everything persists to one PostgreSQL instance; Celery workers driven
  by Celery Beat perform all recurring jobs; model training happens
  offline in a separate pipeline that publishes versioned artifacts the
  Django service layer loads for inference.

  Key principles:
   * PostgreSQL is the single source of truth: raw data, features,
     predictions, odds snapshots, bets, results — everything versioned.
   * Celery Beat drives all recurring work (no cron sprawl).
   * ML training is OFFLINE (scripts/CI), inference is ONLINE (Django
     service layer loads pinned model artifacts). Never train in a request.
   * Every prediction row stores: model version, feature snapshot hash,
     probability vector, and the odds available at prediction time —
     this makes honest backtesting and auditing possible.

3. DATA SOURCES (THE MOAT — BUDGET TIERS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Model quality is capped by data quality. Plan for 3 tiers:

  TIER 0 — FREE (MVP, historical bootstrap):
    * football-data.co.uk        — 25+ seasons CSV, results + closing odds
                                   (Bet365, Pinnacle, market max/avg) for
                                   ~25 leagues. THE backtesting goldmine.
                                   League CSV codes listed in [ODDS §6].
    * football-data.org API      — fixtures, results, standings (free tier).
    * Understat / FBref scraping — xG, shot data (respect robots.txt/ToS;
                                   FBref data is via StatsBomb, check terms).
    * OpenLigaDB, API mirrors    — supplemental.

  TIER 1 — CHEAP PAID (production MVP):
    * API-Football (api-sports.io) — fixtures, lineups, injuries, live
      scores, odds from 20+ books, ~$25-60/mo tiers.
    * The Odds API — clean multi-bookmaker odds feed, good for line
      shopping and closing-line capture.

  TIER 2 — SERIOUS EDGE (later):
    * StatsBomb / Opta / Sportmonks — event-level data, player-level xG
      chains, pressure data. Expensive; only when ROI justifies.
    * Pinnacle API (via broker) — sharpest closing lines = best truth
      signal for calibration benchmarking.

  Ingestion rules:
    * Store RAW payloads (JSONB column) + parsed normalized rows. Raw data
      is immutable; parsers can be re-run when bugs are found.
    * Every entity (team, league, player) gets canonical ID + alias table
      (name matching across sources is the #1 practical pain; build a
      TeamAlias table + fuzzy matcher + manual review queue on day one).
    * Idempotent sync jobs keyed on (source, external_id).

  Data-completeness scoring (v2.0 — gates the pick engine, §8):
    * Every match gets a completeness score in [0,1] computed at feature-
      build time: results history depth, odds coverage (books x markets),
      xG availability, lineup availability, injury-report freshness.
    * Scores are stored on the feature snapshot; the pick engine refuses
      to publish below a per-tier threshold (SAFE stricter than VALUE).
      This is the main defense for the §0.5 thin-data caution: soft-market
      leagues only become bettable when their data actually supports it.

  API budget & rate-limit rules (v2.0):
    * Every client wraps its source with: request budget per day, token-
      bucket rate limiter, exponential backoff + jitter, response cache
      (Redis) with per-endpoint TTL, and a job_run log entry per sync.
    * Odds polling frequency degrades gracefully when budget runs low:
      final-2h polling is protected first, T-7d discovery sacrificed first.
    * Never scrape behind auth or against ToS; prefer paid tiers when a
      source becomes load-bearing (flaky free data poisons everything).

5. FEATURE ENGINEERING (THE REAL DIFFERENTIATOR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  All features computed "as of" kickoff-minus-X, never after. Feature sets
  are versioned (feature_set_version) so models are reproducible.

  5.1 Strength ratings
    * Elo (base + home-advantage term + goal-diff multiplier), K tuned
      per league; separate home/away Elo optionally.
    * Poisson attack/defense strengths (Dixon-Coles with time decay ~0.0065/day
      half-life ≈ backtested per league).
    * Optional: Glicko-2 (uncertainty-aware), pi-ratings.

  5.2 Form & rolling windows (5/10/20-match, exponentially weighted)
    * Points per game, goals for/against, xG for/against (when available),
      shots, shots on target, corners, clean-sheet rate, BTTS rate,
      over-2.5 rate, first-half goal rate.
    * Home-only / away-only splits of all of the above.
    * Rest days, matches in last 14/21 days (fatigue/congestion),
      travel distance (Phase 3), competition context (league vs cup).

  5.3 Head-to-head
    * H2H record last N meetings with time decay; style matchup features
      (possession-vs-counter proxies from shot/possession stats).

  5.4 Squad signals (Phase 2+)
    * Starting XI strength delta vs season average (needs lineups ~1h
      before kickoff — enables a late "lineup-confirmed" re-prediction).
    * Key-player availability index (minutes-weighted), keeper change flag.

  5.5 Market features (extremely powerful — the market is a feature)
    * Opening odds implied probs (de-vig), current odds, line movement
      velocity, sharp-book (Pinnacle) vs soft-book divergence.
    * CAUTION: for VALUE detection, train a market-free model AND a
      market-aware model. The market-aware model is the accuracy champion;
      the market-free model finds disagreements = value candidates.

  5.6 Context
    * League position, points-from-safety/title (motivation proxy),
      season stage, derby flag, weather (Phase 3), referee tendencies
      (cards/penalties markets, Phase 3), manager change recency.

  5.7 Data-quality features (v2.0)
    * The completeness score (§3) itself enters the feature vector — the
      model learns how much to trust thin-data fixtures — and doubles as
      the pick-engine gate (§8). League format flags (playoff-split
      stage for AUT/BEL, dead-rubber flag) also live here.

  De-vig method: use the multiplicative (proportional) method for MVP,
  upgrade to Shin's method (accounts for favourite-longshot bias) — it
  measurably improves implied-probability truth. Library: `shin` (PyPI)
  or implement (closed-form solution exists).

6. MODELING STRATEGY (LAYERED, MARKET BY MARKET)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  6.1 Baselines (build FIRST — they beat naive ML surprisingly often)
    B0: Market baseline — de-vigged closing odds AS the prediction.
        (Any model must beat this on log-loss to claim an edge. Most don't.
         This is the honesty benchmark that keeps the project real.)
    B1: Poisson GLM — goals_home ~ attack_h * defense_a * home_adv;
        derive full scoreline matrix -> every market's probability
        (1X2, O/U, BTTS, DC, DNB, AH, correct score) from ONE model.
    B2: Dixon-Coles — B1 + low-score dependency correction + time decay.
        This 1997 model is still the workhorse of professional soccer
        modeling. Implement carefully; validate against published results.

  6.2 ML layer
    M1: Gradient boosting (LightGBM/XGBoost) multiclass 1X2 on the full
        feature set; monotonic constraints on rating-diff features.
    M2: Separate binary GBMs per derived market (Over 2.5, BTTS, DNB...)
        — often better calibrated than deriving from 1X2.
    M3: Bivariate Poisson / negative binomial regression for goals
        (statsmodels), blended with M1.

  6.3 Ensembling & calibration (where the money is)
    * Stack: logistic meta-learner over {B2, M1, M3, market prob}.
    * Calibrate EVERY final output: isotonic regression (large data) or
      beta calibration (small data), fit on a held-out time slice.
    * Metrics that matter, in order:
        1. Log-loss / Brier vs market baseline B0
        2. Expected Calibration Error (ECE), reliability diagrams
        3. Backtested ROI & max drawdown with realistic staking
        4. CLV (closing line value) — do our picks beat the close?
           Positive CLV is the single best predictor of long-run profit.
      (Plain "accuracy" is reported but NEVER used for model selection.)

  6.4 Training protocol (leakage is the #1 killer — be paranoid)
    * Strictly time-ordered walk-forward splits: train [t0,t1) ->
      validate [t1,t2) -> roll forward. NEVER random K-fold.
    * Per-league models where data allows; pooled model with league
      embeddings/one-hots for small leagues.
    * Retrain cadence: weekly full retrain; ratings (Elo/DC) update daily.
    * Hyperparameter search: Optuna, budgeted, on walk-forward CV only.
    * Every run logged to MLflow: params, metrics, feature importances,
      calibration plots, artifact.

  6.5 Deep learning (Phase 6, optional — only after GBM edge is proven)
    * Sequence models (transformer/GRU over last-N match event vectors),
      player-level graph features. Realistically adds 1-2% log-loss at
      10x complexity; do not start here.

  6.6 League-market cell specialization (v2.0)
    * The unit of edge is the (league, market) CELL, not the league or
      the model: an Eredivisie-Overs specialist and a Serie B-Unders
      specialist will each beat one global everything-model [ODDS §10].
    * Benchmark every cell against B0 to compute its MBI (§0.5). Cells
      are enabled/disabled in market_rule by backtest evidence, not by
      intuition. Draws get NO cell until a Phase 6 specialist model
      exists [ODDS §2 draws note].


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. ODDS ENGINE & "BEST ODDS" SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Poll odds for all tracked fixtures on a schedule: every 6h at T-7d,
    hourly at T-24h, every 5-10 min in the final 2h (rate-limit aware).
  * Line shopping: for each (match, market, selection), keep the BEST
    available decimal odds across bookmakers -> this alone adds 1-3% ROI,
    it is the cheapest real edge in the whole system.
  * Capture the closing line (last snapshot before kickoff, is_closing=TRUE)
    for every market — needed for CLV computation and model benchmarking.
  * De-vig every book's prices (Shin) -> store implied prob per book;
    compute "market consensus prob" weighted toward sharp books.
  * Detect steam moves / sharp divergence as features and as alerts.

  Price-capture timing (v2.0 — full strategy in [ODDS §1C]):
    * Model-liked favorites: capture EARLY (T-3d to T-24h) before public
      money shortens them; popular overs/big-club prices drift the other
      way. Backtest "price at T-24h vs closing" per market to learn each
      market's optimal capture window empirically — store it on the
      market_rule row.
    * Steam-lag route: when a sharp book moves >4-5% and a soft book has
      not followed, the stale soft price is the cheapest edge available;
      surface it as an alert within minutes, not on the next hourly sync.
    * Routing: prefer soft books for value capture, sharps/exchanges for
      volume and account longevity (limits_winners flag drives this).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. VALUE ENGINE, RISK MANAGEMENT & PICK GENERATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  8.1 Value detection
    For each upcoming match, market, selection:
      edge = p_model_calibrated - p_market_devig
      EV   = p_model * (odds_best - 1) - (1 - p_model)
    Publish a VALUE PICK when ALL of:
      * edge >= threshold (start 3-5%, tune by backtest, per market —
        seed values per market in [ODDS §3])
      * p_model within model's proven-calibration range (no extrapolation)
      * odds within the market_rule window; global ceiling 7.0 — hard
        filter, no overrides [ODDS §2]
      * data-completeness score for the match >= threshold (§3)
      * league is whitelisted: latest LPI >= 60 in league_metric
        ([ODDS §5]; leagues at 45-59 are training-data only)
      * model_version is the pinned production version

  8.2 SAFE PICKS tier (the user's "low risk" requirement)
      * Candidate markets: Double Chance, Draw No Bet, Over 0.5/1.5,
        Asian Handicap heavy lines, "favorite to advance".
      * Selection rule: p_model >= 0.85 AND edge >= 1.5% AND odds >= 1.10.
      * Hit-rate target 85-92%; comes with explicit warning that ROI per
        bet is small and one loss wipes several wins — Kelly handles this.

  8.3 Staking (risk control — non-negotiable defaults)
      * Fractional Kelly at 25% (quarter-Kelly): stake = 0.25 * bankroll *
        (p*odds - 1)/(odds - 1), floored at 0, capped at 2% of bankroll.
      * Portfolio caps: max 10% of bankroll open at once; max 4% on one
        match; max 15% on one league per day; correlated-pick detection
        (same match different markets counted as one exposure).
      * Circuit breakers: pause auto-picks if 20-bet rolling CLV < 0, or
        drawdown > 15%, or live calibration ECE degrades 2x from backtest.
      * All parameters live in DB (RiskProfile model), editable in admin.

  8.4 Pick lifecycle
      generated -> published (dashboard/API/notification) -> odds re-checked
      at publish time -> tracked -> settled by results job -> P&L, CLV,
      hit-rate rolled into daily performance report.

  8.5 Thin-week rule (v2.0)
      The system's default answer in a thin week is "fewer picks", never
      "lower thresholds". No calendar pressure, no quota. An empty pick
      list is a correct output. [ODDS §9]


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. BACKTESTING FRAMEWORK (BUILD BEFORE BELIEVING ANYTHING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Vectorized pandas engine over historical matches + historical odds
    (football-data.co.uk closing odds are sufficient for MVP).
  * Walk-forward protocol: for each season S in test range:
      train on all data < S (with decay), predict S, apply the FULL
      pick-generation + staking pipeline, settle, compound bankroll.
  * Must simulate honestly:
      - use odds available at prediction time (or closing as conservative
        proxy), minus a slippage haircut (e.g. 1-2% odds reduction)
      - include void/postponed handling
      - never let features peek past kickoff (assert as-of timestamps)
  * Outputs: equity curve, ROI/bet, yield, max drawdown, hit rate per
    market/league/odds-band, CLV distribution, calibration plots,
    p-value of profit vs luck (bootstrap resampling of bet outcomes).
  * Promotion rule: a model/config ships to production ONLY if it beats
    the current champion on walk-forward log-loss AND backtested
    risk-adjusted return over >= 2 full seasons across >= 3 leagues.

  Per-league decomposition report (v2.0 — writes league_metric rows):
    For every league with >= 2 backtested seasons, report side by side:
      (a) model log-loss            — raw predictability
      (b) closing-line log-loss     — market sharpness
      (c) MBI = (b) - (a)           — harvestable edge (§0.5)
      plus draw rate, O2.5 rate, sample size, and per-cell yield.
    Ranked by MBI, this table IS the league strategy: it settles the
    "low leagues feel more predictable" question with evidence, feeds
    the LPI review, and flips market_rule cells on/off. Expect Big-5
    cells to show good (a) but near-zero (c); expect soft-market league
    cells to show worse (a) but positive (c) — bet (c), not (a).

12. HONEST SUCCESS CRITERIA (WHAT "BEST" LOOKS LIKE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Model: log-loss within 1-2% of (or better than) de-vigged closing
    line; live ECE < 0.03; SAFE tier hit rate >= 85% over 200+ picks.
  * Economics: positive CLV on >= 55% of picks; backtested and
    paper-traded yield of 3-8% per bet on VALUE tier; max drawdown < 20%
    at quarter-Kelly.
  * League strategy (v2.0): every enabled (league, market) cell justified
    by a positive-MBI, positive-yield backtest over >= 2 seasons; no cell
    enabled on intuition; the /leagues page can explain WHY any league is
    in or out.
  * Engineering: full audit trail from any pick back to raw data, odds
    snapshot, feature snapshot, and model version; zero-leakage verified
    by tests; retrain-to-production is one reviewed click.
  * Discipline: the system refuses to bet when it has no edge. Most days
    the best pick list is SHORT. That restraint — not accuracy — is what
    separates profitable systems from losing ones.

13. FAILURE MODES & ANTI-PATTERNS (v2.0 — HOW THESE SYSTEMS ACTUALLY DIE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. DATA LEAKAGE — the #1 killer. A feature quietly computed from
     post-kickoff data makes backtests spectacular and live betting
     ruinous. Defense: frozen feature snapshots, as-of assertions in
     tests (§10.5), suspicion of ANY too-good result.
  2. BACKTEST OVERFITTING — tuning thresholds until history looks great.
     One season, one league, 200 bets = curve-fitting, not evidence.
     Defense: walk-forward only, >= 2 seasons x >= 3 leagues promotion
     rule, bootstrap p-value on profit, out-of-sample holdout seasons.
  3. ODDS AVAILABILITY BIAS — backtesting on best-of-market odds you
     could never have actually taken. Defense: slippage haircut (§9),
     closing-line-as-proxy conservatism, live paper-trade comparison.
  4. IGNORING NEGATIVE CLV — bankroll up, CLV down = running on luck.
     The bankroll lies for months; CLV tells the truth in weeks.
     Defense: CLV circuit breaker (§8.3) trumps the equity curve.
  5. CHASING THE TAIL — model shows an 8% edge on a 9.0 longshot; it is
     almost always model miscalibration in the tail. Defense: 7.0 hard
     ceiling, calibration-range gate (§8.1).
  6. QUOTA PRESSURE — publishing picks because subscribers/users expect
     picks today. Defense: thin-week rule (§8.5); an empty list is a
     feature, not a bug.
  7. SOFT-DATA MIRAGE — a "predictable" low league that is really a
     thin-data artifact or worse, a fixed one (§0.5). Defense:
     completeness gate, integrity auto-blacklist, MBI computed only on
     adequate sample sizes.
  8. STAKING DRIFT — manually overriding Kelly after wins ("we're hot")
     or doubling after losses (Martingale instinct). Defense: stakes are
     computed, capped, and logged; manual bets recorded via API are
     flagged separately in P&L.
  9. SILENT MODEL ROT — league regime changes (dominant era ends, format
     changes) degrade a model that keeps predicting yesterday's league.
     Defense: quarterly LPI/MBI review (§10.2), live-vs-backtest ECE
     monitor, champion/challenger always running.
 10. INFRA DEBT AT THE WORST TIME — odds polling dies silently on
     matchday, picks publish from stale prices. Defense: job_run audit
     rows, Sentry alerts on missed beats, publish-time odds re-check.

14. GLOSSARY (v2.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1X2            Match result market: Home win (1), Draw (X), Away win (2).
  AH             Asian Handicap — goal-start lines (incl. quarter lines);
                 heavy favorite lines are a SAFE-tier staple.
  BTTS           Both Teams To Score (Yes/No).
  CLV            Closing Line Value — the % by which your taken odds beat
                 the closing odds. The best long-run profit predictor.
  DC             Double Chance (1X or X2) — sells draw risk cheaply.
  De-vig         Removing the bookmaker margin from odds to recover the
                 market's true implied probabilities (Shin's method here).
  DNB            Draw No Bet — stake refunded on a draw.
  ECE            Expected Calibration Error — average gap between claimed
                 probability and observed frequency.
  Edge           p_model_calibrated - p_market_devig for a selection.
  EV             Expected Value per unit staked:
                 p*(odds-1) - (1-p). Positive EV = value bet.
  Kelly          Bankroll-fraction staking formula maximizing log growth;
                 we use quarter-Kelly (25%) capped at 2% of bankroll.
  LPI            League Predictability Index — 0-100 weighted league score
                 (imbalance, draws, stability, churn, data, softness,
                 integrity). Defined in [ODDS §5]; >= 60 = bettable.
  MBI            Market Beatability Index — closing-line log-loss minus
                 model log-loss, per league or per (league, market) cell.
                 Positive = harvestable edge (§0.5). The pick engine
                 follows MBI, not raw accuracy.
  O/U            Over/Under total-goals markets (0.5 / 1.5 / 2.5 lines).
  Steam move     Sudden sharp-book line move from informed money; lagging
                 soft-book prices become briefly exploitable.
  Vig/Margin     The bookmaker's built-in profit: implied probabilities
                 summing above 100%.
  xG             Expected Goals — shot-quality-based scoring model; the
                 strongest publicly available team-strength signal.
